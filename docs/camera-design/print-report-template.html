01 / Material decision

Value or presentation?

Both choices suit a functional prototype. The first build should prove the fits and camera geometry before paying for premium cosmetic finishing.

Best practical value candidate

Black PETG · FDM

A tough filament print for initial assembly and testing. A local shop gives you a chance to resolve fit issues without another international shipment.

AppearanceVisible layer lines; typically a glossier surface.
StrengthSuitable for mechanical parts. Mast and pivot orientation must account for layer direction.
Quote basis0.20 mm layers, four perimeters and 30% infill as starting settings; shop review required.
Budget$75–$175 estimated; target a competitive local offer around $100–$150.

Material basis: Prusa PETG guidance. Settings and buying target are design recommendations, not tested performance or advertised local prices.

Best finish / strength balance

Black nylon · MJF PA12 / PA12S

A consistent matte texture with good mechanical properties across print directions. PA12S is intended to improve the appearance of visible parts.

AppearanceFine, slightly granular finish. Vapor smoothing is an optional extra, not included by default.
StrengthGood choice for housings and brackets. Real load and clamp tests are still needed.
Quote basisDepowdered and black-finished; ask for smoothing as a separate option.
BudgetUnquoted. “From $1” advertising is not a price for this device.

Material basis: HP materials and JLC3DP PA12S specifications.

Fit comes before finish. The base socket has only 0.3 mm clearance per side. JLC3DP lists ±0.3 mm tolerance for dimensions within 100 mm, so combined errors can consume the clearance. Ask for a mating-fit review; the small fastener coupon alone does not validate the sliding mast. Keep the two optical diffusers out of the black-plastic order.

03 / Order size

Where the plastic goes.

The base and mast make up more than half the model volume. These are the main areas to review for strength, print orientation and cost.

One set means one of each of 13 STL files. The two ballast caps and two light trays are already separate files. No tray or purchased electronics are included.

Largest features:
Base: 160 × 120 × 65 mm
Tray support: 100 × 210 × 3 mm
Upper mast/yoke: 42 × 24 × 211 mm
Individual parts fit a nominal 220 × 220 mm bed before brims, with at least 211 mm usable Z in the supplied orientation.

06 / Recommended next step

Get comparable quotes, then print.

  1. Request local PETG pricing first. Compare 3D By Design and Hot Nozzle using the same 13-part package. A complete offer near $100–$150 is worth considering if cleanup and timing meet your needs.
  2. Price the nylon upgrade. Request black MJF PA12/PA12S, with smoothing separate. Confirm mating fits before choosing a premium finish.
  3. Validate before the full build. Print the fastener coupon and resolve tube/socket fit, orientation and actual camera coverage. Buy the full print only after those checks.
