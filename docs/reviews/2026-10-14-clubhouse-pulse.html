A proposed motion language

Anticipation → commitment → payoff.

The scoreframe is the anchor. Review builds anticipation; only an accepted score earns the motion. The score remains readable throughout, and the next action stays available.

01 / The everyday landing

A frame compresses, settles, and releases one ring. The total gets one upward tick. No confetti for ordinary points.

Proposed timing · 450–800 ms

02 / Category choreography

Full House assembles 3 + 2. Straights build in order. Four of a Kind gathers a matching set. These are category emblems, not observations of actual dice.

Proposed timing · 700–1,500 ms

03 / The rare headline

Yahtzee has gold accents, five dice and a contained burst. Upper bonus has its own +35 reveal. Larger finish sequences are a later design slice.

Proposed timing · 1,200–2,200 ms

04 / A graceful low score

A zero is recorded clearly. No loss shake, failure siren or mocking visual. “Next frame, fresh chance” keeps the evening friendly.

Neutral feedback · no reward burst

05 / Sound as punctuation

Short optional synthetic chimes audition the rhythm. Production audio needs its own listening review, mute control and coordination with planned spoken commentary.

Sound off by default · no paid services

06 / The honest quiet moments

Undo and correction update the sheet quietly. Production needs accepted-event deduplication, hidden-total protection and quiet reload/reconnect behavior.

Reduced motion keeps text and color
