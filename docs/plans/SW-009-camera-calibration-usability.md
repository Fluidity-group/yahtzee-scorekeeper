## Registration proposal and current limits

Current manual polygon is fixed in image coordinates. Keep camera and tray rigidly fixed relative to each other, and recalibrate after moving either. Stream dimension/session changes invalidate calibration, but current software does not detect every physical tray/camera movement; don't claim an automatic movement interlock. Camera and tray need not remain fixed forever in a future tracking design. During a throw/capture/review, stable geometry is still required. Any detected geometry change must discard pending review, stop capture acceptance, reacquire a stable outline and ask confirmation; it must never silently apply a new crop to an old observation.

Candidate stages: (1) automatic blue/contrasting octagonal-outline proposal with explicit confirmation and manual fallback; (2) coded-marker registration with known tray geometry, confidence/visibility checks and movement detection; (3) qualified reacquisition after tray/camera movement. All remain proposed, not implemented. Blue/outline-only recognition can be confused by dice, hands, shadows and similarly coloured surfaces. Pixel outline/scale is possible without physical measurements; true millimetres require known marker size or tray dimensions. Marker detection isn't automatically lens calibration, nor does a homography alone remove lens distortion.

Recommend removable printed coded markers (ArUco/AprilTag family subject to runtime evaluation) with distinct IDs, rigidly attached around the outer perimeter, excluded from dice recognition. A known multi-marker layout can recover planar registration and orientation; some occlusion can be tolerated when enough well-spread correspondences remain, otherwise pause. Markers attached to the table cannot track a tray independently sliding on it. Marker placement must account for the height difference between raised rim and rolling floor: use coplanar flat border/tabs where possible, or model the rim/floor offset and lens geometry. Measure readability at the actual camera distance/angle and stream resolution before specifying print dimensions.

White dots at eight corners are a useful manual targeting aid, but identical dots have no encoded identity/orientation and can resemble dice pips. Prefer unobtrusive numbered/notched corner accents for manual targeting, or distinct coded registration marks for automatic detection. Use temporary removable matte stickers for trials; retain smooth rolling surface and avoid printing pip-like patterns in the recognised dice area. Additional non-lighting aids: nonslip tray base, repeatable camera mount/position stops, clear matte floor-to-wall boundary, known tray dimensions, and a reproducible empty-tray reference.

Future custom tray: integrate registration into a flat decorative perimeter outside playable area, surveyed against the inside eight-corner outline; optionally provide a known-layout removable calibration insert for initial lens/geometry setup. An insert removed afterward cannot by itself detect later tray movement. Actual marker family/runtime availability, optical size, occlusion limits, latency, false alignment, moved-tray rejection and independent review are open. No purchase, permanent tray modification or automatic game command authorized/inferred.

Primary references: [OpenCV ArUco boards](https://docs.opencv.org/4.7.0/db/da9/tutorial_aruco_board_detection.html) for identified marker layouts/pose and partial visibility; [OpenCV planar homography](https://docs.opencv.org/5.0/tutorials/features/homography/homography.html) for mapping known planar geometry. Recommendations above are engineering proposals applying those methods, not performance evidence from this C121.
